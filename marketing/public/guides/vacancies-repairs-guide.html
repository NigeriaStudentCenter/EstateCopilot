1

Vacancies

Got an empty unit? Here's who does what to get it seen — and rented.

Who

The landlord

Only the landlord (or their property manager) can list a vacancy. It's done from their own Properties page.

How

One toggle

Flip Advertise on public site on for that property — no separate form.

What happens next

It goes public

The unit appears on EstateCopilot's public listings page, and anyone can request a viewing.

1. Landlord

Adds the property with photos & a description

2. Landlord

Turns on "Advertise on public site"

3. Public site

Unit appears on the state's listings page

4. Visitor

Picks a date & time, books a viewing

5. Landlord

Confirms or cancels it in Bookings

1a. Listing a vacant unit — step by step

  1. Open the property you want to advertise

    In the landlord portal, go to Properties and find the unit that's empty. If it's not added yet, add it first (title, address, LGA, rent, deposit) — see the Landlord Guide's Adding & managing your properties section.

  2. Add good photos and a clear description

    This is what visitors judge the unit by before they even book a viewing — clear, honest photos and a short description of what makes it a good place to live.

  3. Turn on "Advertise on public site"

    Find the toggle on the property's card and switch it on. That's it — the unit is now live.

    TipFound a tenant? Flip the same toggle back off to pull the listing down immediately.

  4. See it from the visitor's side

    Anyone can browse estatecopilot.org/properties, filter by state, and open your listing. They pick a date and time and fill in their name, phone and email to request a viewing — no account needed on their end.

1b. Managing viewing requests

  1. You're notified straight away

    The moment someone books a viewing, you get a WhatsApp message and an email — you don't need to be watching the dashboard for it to reach you.

  2. Open Bookings

    Every request lands in Bookings, grouped by date like a calendar.

  3. Confirm or cancel

    Tap Confirm if the time works, or Cancel if it doesn't — then follow up with the visitor directly using the phone number shown.

2

Repair requests

Something's broken. Here's who reports it, who fixes it, and how everyone knows where things stand.

Who

The tenant starts it

Only the tenant living in the unit reports a repair — from their own tenant portal.

How

A quick checklist

They tick the closest matching issue and describe it in a sentence.

What happens next

The landlord takes it from there

It lands in their Maintenance page, ready to handle directly or send to an artisan.

1. Tenant

Ticks a checklist item & describes the issue

2. System

Marks it Landlord's or Tenant's responsibility

3. Landlord

Sees the ticket, fixes it directly — or lists it on the marketplace

4. Artisan

(if listed) quotes or books a site visit

5. Marked complete

Landlord or tenant confirms it's fixed

2a. How a tenant reports a repair

  1. Open Maintenance in the tenant portal

    Tap the Maintenance tab.

  2. Tick the checklist item that matches

    Items are grouped into 🏠 Landlord's responsibility (structural things — plumbing, wiring, the roof) and 🔧 Your responsibility (day-to-day upkeep). Ticking one shows you instantly which side of the line your issue falls on — before you even submit it. Not sure? Tick "Something else / not sure" instead.

  3. Describe the issue and submit

    Add a short, clear description — e.g. "Generator won't start, water pooling near the meter box" — then tap Report issue.

  4. Track it

    Your report now appears in a list below the form, with a status badge that updates as your landlord works on it: OPEN → DISPATCHED → AWAITING TENANT SIGNOFF → RESOLVED.

2b. How a landlord manages it

  1. Open Maintenance in the landlord portal

    Every ticket shows the property, the tenant's description, the responsibility badge, and its status.

  2. Handle it yourself, or send it out

    For a small job you'll fix yourself or with your own guy, just get it done and tap Mark complete when it's finished. For anything you'd rather hand to a professional, keep reading — that's the marketplace, covered next.

  3. Mark it complete

    Once the job is done, tap Mark complete. If the tenant already confirmed it from their side, you'll see "✓ Tenant signed off" instead.

2c. Sending it to the artisan marketplace

Don't have your own repair guy for this job? EstateCopilot has a public marketplace of artisans who can quote for it directly.

  1. List the ticket on the marketplace

    On the ticket, tap List on marketplace. The job is now visible publicly on estatecopilot.org/handymen — showing only the LGA, state, category and description. Your exact address is never shown until you've accepted a quote.

  2. Artisans respond

    An artisan either submits a quote straight away, or books a site visit first if they need to see the job to price it accurately. Either way, you're notified the same way as a viewing request — WhatsApp and email.

  3. Compare quotes and accept one

    Tap View quotes on the ticket to see every offer. When you're ready, tap Accept on the one you want — this records the artisan's contact details and dispatches the job.

    TipNo rush to accept — leave a job listed for a day or two to collect a few quotes before choosing.
Ownership, in one lineTenants report. Landlords decide and manage. Artisans only ever see what you choose to publish, and only after you've listed a job.
