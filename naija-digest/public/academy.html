The cognitive trap

Most kids are using AI the exact wrong way.

They type a question, copy the answer, and hand it in. That doesn’t just trip plagiarism filters — it stops them thinking. They aren’t learning the technology. They’re becoming dependent on it.

The shortcut — how most kids use AI

  1. Paste the homework question into a chatbot.
  2. Copy the answer, tidy a few words, submit.
  3. Plagiarism flag — or a teacher who knows it isn’t their voice.
  4. No idea how the answer was reached. Can’t reproduce it in an exam.
  5. Every new task, the same reflex: ask the machine.

The Socratic Shift — what we teach on day one

  1. The AI is set up as a patient tutor that refuses to give the answer.
  2. It breaks the topic down and asks one diagnostic question at a time.
  3. Every claim the student makes has to be checked against a real source.
  4. When they get it right, they have to explain why it works.
  5. They leave able to do it again — on paper, in the exam hall, alone.

We fix this on day one. It’s a single change in how the tool is pointed — and it turns AI from a crutch into the most patient teacher your child has ever had.

Parent peace of mind

Four rules we enforce on every single submission.

This isn’t a hope. It’s a graded code of conduct — work that breaks it is handed back, not marked.

The 40% Human RuleStudents must manually rewrite, expand or critique at least 40% of any AI-assisted draft. If they can’t explain a sentence, it doesn’t go in.
Source-FirstNo fact from an AI enters their work without a second source the student found — a textbook, a named site, a teacher.
100% Privacy SandboxTraining happens inside enterprise-grade private workspaces. Their data is never used to train public AI models.
Declare the helpStudents tell their teacher exactly how AI helped — to plan, to check grammar, to make flashcards. Honesty is the whole point.
